# Alternative EM List description

[The Engineering Leader’s Checklist](../README.md)

Hey there! *adjusts imaginary tie*
So picture this: There I was, preparing for job interviews like any normal person with an unhealthy fear of blanking mid-conversation, when I had what I'd like to call a "slightly less terrible than usual" idea. After spending about 8 years bouncing around various engineering leadership roles – you know, the kind where you try to explain to executives why "just adding more developers" isn't actually going to make the project go faster – I decided to create what I'm modestly calling the "Please Don't Let Me Forget Basic Leadership Stuff During Interviews" framework.
Why did I make this? Well, I got tired of having those post-interview moments where you're in your car, banging your head against the steering wheel, suddenly remembering EVERYTHING you should have said. You know the feeling – it's like remembering the perfect comeback... three years later in the shower.
But here's the plot twist: While creating this totally-not-obsessive list of everything an engineering leader needs to worry about (spoiler alert: it's a lot), I realized something. This would have been insanely helpful when I was first stepping into leadership, back when I thought making everyone happy all the time was not only possible but probably in my job description (spoiler: it's not, and your stress levels will thank you for learning that one early).
So what is this exactly? Think of it as your friendly neighborhood checklist of "Oh right, I should probably think about that" moments. It's not going to solve world hunger or magically make your project estimates accurate (I know, I'm still crying about that too), but it will remind you of all those areas you need to care about – whether you want to or not.
And yes, some of these responsibilities overlap with Product and Delivery folks, because apparently, we can't have nice, clean boundaries in software engineering. Who knew?
Who needs this? Well:
* Fresh-faced engineering leaders who are wondering what they've gotten themselves into
* Veterans preparing for interviews who, like me, don't trust their brain to work under pressure
* Engineering leaders joining a new company who want to look like they've got their act together from day one

Consider it my contribution to making the world of engineering leadership slightly less chaotic. *winks at camera*
And yes, I threw in some resources because I'm nice like that. You're welcome, internet.

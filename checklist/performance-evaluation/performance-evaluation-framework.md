# Performance Evaluation Framework
# What do we want to achieve?
Have a **clear overview of individual performance** across the team, so that we’re able to use it as a reference point for business decisions like reward alignment, spotting pain points, addressing and counteracting underperformance and properly managing individual growth and recognizing superstars.
### What do we need to achieve that?
For making good decisions in the above areas we need clear, comparable, fair and comparable data across the team. In order to build that dataset we need:
* **regular points in time** when we gather comprehensive assessment (including a track record over longer period
* fair, unified **set of expectations** (aka. career map)
* diverse set of **insights on behaviours** against those expectations (self-reflection, manager’s evaluation, 360 feedback)
* unified **scale of assessment**
* ability to **calibrate the assessments** across the organization for fairness

⠀Regular Performance Snapshots
One of the first traits of our dataset needs to be regularity. Moreover, if we want it to be fair and comparable, it should be the same frequency across the team. At the same time we need to be careful not to overdo it - e.g. doing full evaluation across all the expectations is not scalable nor sustainable, and can cause fatigue and bureaucracy, leading to poor quality results. On the other hand, we want as detailed and useful data as we possibly can, so that we don’t end up trying to figure out what was that thing 6 months ago I wanted to highlight.
In order to find the sweet spot between the two we can split those efforts into separate threads
* regular gathering of insight and targeted, adhoc performance support -> nosurprises -> alwayson
* regular performance evaluations to generate consistent track record -> aligned -> fair -> comprehensive

⠀❗️👉 “Always on” and regular formal evaluations are not mutually exclusive, they complete one another! 👈❗️
### 💚 “Always on” aka. nosurprises
This happens in 1:1s - an employee and their manager regularly discuss any performance related insights as they go, whenever it’s relevant. That can be both recognitions, to capture what’s going well or even above and beyond, as well as things that need support and getting back on track.
In those 1:1s, manager’s role is to coach their employees through there situations (help them name and frame them), provide support (resources, mentoring, proper context etc.) and coach them through acting on it (leverage the good stuff, encourage and/or help getting the right feedback, coach planning and actioning the plans etc.).
Important part is keeping track record of all those situations, which will enable making more informed and quality assessments in the less frequent, but more comprehensive performance evaluations.
### 🎭 Performance Snapshot
This is the event that would look at all the gathered evidence and create an assessment of performance in time (for given period). The frequency of the period needs to be well adjusted so it’s not too rare (e.g. only annual) and not to often (e.g. every 2 weeks or even a month).
If it’s too rarely (e.g. annual):
* for most people it’s **too long period to reflect on properly** (distance in time)
* it produces **to few datapoints** (i.e. might mean you need to wait year/half-year to use that for context)

⠀If it’s too often (e.g. every 2 weeks or every month)
* **creates fatigue**, which has a high risk of low engagement and poor quality
* **not enough time for most actions to take effect** between periods and create a tangible difference (”I’m still working on it”, “I didn’t have time to do it yet”, “It’s still the same”) - bearing in mind this is for a comprehensive assessment, not for targeted insights covered in always-on/no-surprises part.

⠀**❓So how often to do this ❓**
* ~3 months apart
* fixed frequency for all
* aligned with a relevant seasonality for the organization (most default typical quarters, but it can be natural seasons, high/low peaks seasons etc.)

⠀**What goes into such a Performance Snapshot?** *(ALL IN CONTEXT OF SPECIFIC PERIOD)*
* **Employees self-reflection**
  * **applying a unified scale** (1-4, doesn’t meet exp./meets exp./exceeds exp., etc.) to given frame at some level of abstraction (each expectations or each group of expectations or even overall performance - the more generic, the smoother the process)
  * providing insights to their evaluation (e.g. giving top 3 examples to support their evaluations - this is where the “always on” kicks in and helps with that)
  * (optional) what support they need for the next period
* **Managers evaluation** (same as above just from managerial perspective)
* **360 feedback -** asking people around the individual to provide insights on their impact (on them, on the team)
* **Calibration** - Managers meet and discuss the rankings, to make sure that they fairly apply the same scale to all (e.g. 4’s/exceeds expectations means the same and we don’t end up giving them away like candy in one place for single peaks and/or it being next to impossible to get with another manager)
* **Feedback to the employee** - Manager plays back the final score, justification and discuss any actions that might come out of that with their report.

⠀Why to go through all this trouble? Where can it be used?
* **💰 Reward / Salary Review**
  * if an employee expresses the need, we have the data we need to review that at any point in time
  * we can have regular periods where we might review salaries across the board to take proper actions due to market changes, proactive recognitions, pay fairness etc.
* **🤩 Promotion -** we can have fair, structured and comprehensive data to act on any promotion at any point in time
* **🛟 Underperformance management** - minor incidents are to be spotted and acted on throughout “always on” approach, however there might be patters that call for more decisive or structured actions (e.g. putting someone on a PIP if there’s a pattern of low performance snapshots)

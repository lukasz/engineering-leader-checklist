# Yourself 🤗

[The Engineering Leader’s Checklist](../README.md)

On the plane, during the safety instructions announcements, they always tell you to put your oxygen mask first and then to assist anyone who might need help. The same applies here! You can only be the support for your team they deserve if you are fit to do so - therfore you need to take care of yourself. 

## Checklist
- **Burnout** - watch out for both symptoms of burnout and all the common factors that might lead to it:
  - The temptation to take on other people’s work 
  - Meeting fatigue 
  - Watch out for overtime.
- **Imposter syndrome** - 
- **Time Management** - as a leader, you’ll be swamped with an endless chain of meetings - don’t let that overrun you. Save time - by literally blocking it out in your calendar - for:
  - **focus work** - write down that feedback, go through your notes, get through that report, read that PR, and whatever else your role requires you to do but you struggle to squeeze that in between meetings.
  - **reading** - you need to stay up to date - articles, blogs, books, etc. - not just tech-related, but also around management and leadership (e.g. Harvard Business Review has the same really good content) this is crucial to your role to stay up to date and trends as much as keeping up with tech for developers.
  - **reflection** - save some time to literally stop and think about things. 
- **Get a Coach** - a good coach can help you to figure things out 10x faster. They’re not there to provide a solution; they’re there to be the catalyst for your own reasoning. 
- **Get a Mentor** - a good mentor will not only share invaluable knowledge with you, but they will help you to leverage your experience and get so much more learnings from what you do day to day. 
- **Transitioning from IC to MG role**
  - It’s a lateral move onto a parallel track, not a promotion, you’ll be junior in some key skills
  - Your work now has a much longer execution time - results will come in a day(s), weeks(s) or event month(s) 
  - You will get imposter syndrome - it’s ok, just don’t let it eat you up. 
- **Ask for feedback regularly** - it can be anything casual in 1:1 conversations to formal, tailored 360 feedback designed to provide you with insights into the areas you want to work on.

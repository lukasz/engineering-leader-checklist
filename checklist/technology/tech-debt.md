# Tech Debt

[The Engineering Leader’s Checklist](../../README.md) -> [Technology 🦾](../technology.md)

Technical Debt represents the implied cost and consequences of choosing a limited or expedient technical solution now, over a better but more time-consuming approach. It's like financial debt - you gain something now (speed to market, meeting a deadline) but you'll have to "pay it back" with interest (additional work) later. 
Much like with actual debt in business, it’s actually good to have a healthy amount to drive your product forward - but it has to be managed. 

### Checklist
- **Metrics** - make sure you measure your Tech Debt - there are many ways you can do it, often depending on the nature of the product, but the key traits are to look at:
  - **Impact of the existing Tech Debt** - eg. the number of incidents, bugs etc.
  - **Impact of the paid-off Tech Debt** - eg. improved performance metrics, DORA metrics etc.
- **Continuous Payments** - much like a mortgage, you want to pay your Tech Debt regularly by making a dedicated time for it in your planning and making sure it’s part of how you work on your daily tasks (i.e. applying The Boyscout Rule** - „Leave the codebase cleaner than you found it” - make small improvements/payments whenever you get a chance as part of what you’re working on) 
- **Healthy amount of Tech Debt** - not all Tech Debt is bad, nor should you aim at having none of it. The threshold will depend on a few factors like the product or the industry - but ultimately, some of it is not worth addressing. It’s important to keep that in mind. Unless you can call out specific value fixing it brings - you probably should leave it alone.
